# Combat data and motion

The engine is a CPU-only simulation. `src/game/combat/moves.ts` owns the typed `MoveDefinition`: stable ID, behavior ID, pose identity, integer 60 Hz startup/active/recovery, shape trajectory and dimensions, damage/knockback, and cancel windows. `playerMove` resolves all six weapon styles, combo stage, charge and rolled normal module. `specialMove` resolves every advertised special slot. Labels are display text; neither simulation nor rendering dispatches on `attack.name`.

`update` prepares attack timing and velocities, integrates actors, resolves floor/props/grabs/depth rails, then checks collisions. `AttackVisual.collision` stores the exact checked world capsule, and rendering consumes that snapshot. Moving phase/dash/falling aerial browser regressions compare it to the actual mesh rather than recomputing a render-only pose.

`beginMove` creates one swing record with a per-target hit set. The engine and renderer consume the same resolved definition. Projectiles have separate ownership, hit memory, lifetime and pierce budgets; they do not reuse the melee capsule. A projectile confirms only its originating swing while that exact swing is still current; a delayed old shot cannot grant the next move a hit cancel. A missed grab is an explicit non-hitting `none` behavior with zero active frames, never a distant timing sentinel.

## Coordinate and geometry contract

World +X is stage right, +Y is up, +Z is the front lane. Facing mirrors only X. `sampleWeapon` returns world-space capsule endpoints for any frame of a move and is the single source of weapon geometry for simulation, rendering and the skeleton. Inside the hit window (`startup ≤ frame ≤ activeEnd`) it computes the strike arc; for every move without an authored arc this is the same arithmetic as before, so hit detection is bit-identical. Outside it, the segment comes from `weaponPose` in `trajectory.ts`: the authored wind-up and follow-through (see [Weapon trajectory and body motion](#weapon-trajectory-and-body-motion)). The engine tests contact only in the hit window, so those frames never hit anything; they live on the simulation side only so every consumer draws the same weapon.

**Up and down arcs.** Every up/down tilt, smash and aerial of the six player weapons carries `shape.arc` (`StrikeArc` in `moves.ts`), which replaces `shape.trajectory` on every frame of the move. On hit frames `sampleWeapon` places the pivot (the hands) at `x = actor.x + facing·0.3`, `z = actor.z`, with its height travelling linearly from `pivotY[0]` to `pivotY[1]` across the window, and lays a segment of length `reach·(0.75 + 0.25·sin πt)` along `arcAim(shape, t)`, the angle running from `from` to `to`:

- `vertical`: the angle is the elevation from the facing direction (0 forward, π/2 straight up, π behind, negative below). The aim has no Z component, so a vertical arc stays exactly in the fighter's own lane plane: it changes elevation and never repurposes depth.
- `level`: the angle is a heading (0 forward, positive toward the near lane +Z) with a constant vertical component `tilt`; Z is compressed by `min(1, depth/reach)` as for a slash. A level sweep deliberately covers depth: that is how grounded down attacks reach the neighbouring lanes.

Facing mirrors X only, as for every other move. Jab, dash, forward/back/neutral normals, all specials, enemy and utility moves have no arc and are unchanged. The upgrades `pile-launch` (down smash becomes a `burst` shockwave) and `lance-sweep` (down tilt becomes an `orbit`) delete the arc and use their original trajectories.

The lance has a narrow shaft/tip capsule and cannot hit a rear fighter on a forward thrust. On hit frames it extends to full reach and retracts along the strike arc; before and after, it draws back and settles to its carried length. Its 1.35 multiplier requires contact on the final 24% of the capsule, including vertical attacks; horizontal distance alone is insufficient.

`geometry.ts` measures exact segment-to-body-box distance. Body bounds include the full scaled torso/head height of the boss.

**Lane assist (奥・手前の補正).** The player's attacks are forgiving across lanes; enemy attacks are not. `src/game/combat/assist.ts` owns `PLAYER_LANE_ASSIST` (0.45 world units per side) and the pure `laneAssist(box, pad)`, which widens a box along Z only. The engine applies it when the attacker is the player: melee strikes against enemy body boxes and destructible props, and player-owned projectiles (pulse bolts, cannon shots, echo shots) against enemy bodies, whose Z test gains the same 0.45. X reach and height are untouched, so a target just beyond the weapon's X reach or above its capsule is still missed. Every forward tilt therefore reaches 0.45 further into the neighbouring lane than before (lance 0.42 → 0.87, razor 0.58 → 1.03, cannon 0.71 → 1.16 at x = 1.4). Enemy strikes, enemy projectiles, mortars and floor hazards test the player's exact, unpadded body. A projectile still stops at a prop on its unpadded footprint, so the assist never lets cover catch more of the player's shots. Sweeps clamp to the active interval, sample adaptively according to endpoint travel / capsule radius, and check endpoint edges. Prior actor transforms refresh before every integration step, including windup. Collision is independent of the GPU. The shape is an authored gameplay approximation, not per-triangle skeletal collision.

The player has one weapon model, always visible: `render/armory.ts` builds it along local +Y in metres from the pivot (y = 0) to the tip (y = segment length), and `WeaponView` in `render/weapon.ts` lays it on the segment it is handed. On hit frames that is the checked capsule itself; otherwise it is the segment the skeleton carries or winds up (see [The held weapon](#the-held-weapon)). The tip mesh ends exactly at the segment's end. The weapon view never decides where the weapon is. The skeleton follows the same segment: IK places the gripping hand on it, and no arm is scaled to stay connected (see [Skeleton and motion](#skeleton-and-motion)). Trails use these endpoints. Centered debug/trail cubes are positioned at the segment midpoint. Browser tests compare the actual Pixi world matrix of the tip, trail and debug endpoints against numerical simulation samples for mirrored forward/back/up/down lance aerials. The test measures geometry, not just timing or shared function calls.

Training's **HITBOX / 判定表示** button shows active weapon segments/endpoints and body heights in every graphics preset. The green thick line represents the capsule axis/radius; it is not a full translucent body hull. Startup/recovery have no live weapon overlay. Enemy body panels are drawn with the lane assist's depth — the depth the player's weapon is tested against — and the player's own panel is the exact box enemy attacks test.

## Skeleton and motion

Each humanoid `RobotView` is a set of rigid bones: hips, chest, head, upper arms, forearms, thighs, shins and feet. The bones are direct children of the robot root; every frame the CPU-solved skeleton sets each bone's position and quaternion in rig space. Bone lengths are constant and no mesh is scaled to reach a target. Drones keep their simple non-skeletal bob and rotor animation.

The rig layer, `src/render/rig/`, is renderer-free TypeScript and runs in Node unit tests. Rig space is the actor's own unscaled space: +Z forward, +Y up, +X the robot's right hand, with `rig = Ry(−yaw)·(world − actor)/scale` (Y is measured from `actor.y`). `RigSpec` (`skeleton.ts`) holds the joint layout and mirrors the box meshes in `RobotView`; heavy and boss kinds are wider.

`RigAnimator.update` runs once per frame per actor:

1. **Authored pose** (`poses.ts`). `statePose` returns named scalar channels (`CHANNELS`). Hands are offsets from their shoulder in the chest frame; feet are rig-space sole targets; `plant` says whether the feet should be anchored; the `carry*` channels re-aim the weapon carried between attacks. The `dodge` and `airdodge` poses lean into the measured direction of travel (`heading` from the gait velocity, in the rig's own frame): pitched forward or back along the facing, rolled and stepping out toward a lane; a dodge on the spot reads as forward. There is no wavedash pose (the state is gone). Attack poses come from `strikePose` in `choreography.ts` (see [Weapon trajectory and body motion](#weapon-trajectory-and-body-motion)); `attackMotion` in `motion.ts` now feeds only VFX (the trail window and the enemy slash arc), never the body.
2. **Blending.** A new state, a different move, an airborne change or a restarted attack inertializes from the previous output: the difference to the new pose decays over a short state-dependent time (`blendTime`, about 0.03–0.16 s). Hip yaw takes the short way round. `plant` is never blended. The chest's turn rate and a free hand's target speed are bounded, so a two-frame wind-up cannot snap them.
3. **Foot planting** (`gait.ts`). `FootPlanter` measures real displacement, so knockback, dashes and conveyors all count as movement; a teleport-sized jump releases the anchors instead of producing a stride. Standing feet stay anchored in world space and take corrective steps one at a time when they drift from the pose. Moving feet follow a phased run cycle whose cadence and duty follow measured speed; each foot lands ahead so it passes under the hip in mid-stance. Airborne, sliding (dash, rush, phase, leap), dodge, hurt, spin and mounted poses drive the feet directly. A pitched foot rolls about its ground contact — the heel at strike, the toe at push-off — so the contact point never slides; a stance foot left out of the leg's reach lifts early.
4. **Solve** (`skeleton.ts`). `solveRig` applies FK for hips, chest and head, sinks the hips until planted legs can reach their anchors, then solves arms and legs with analytic two-bone IK (`ik.ts`) and pole vectors from the pose. An out-of-reach target moves the end joint along the root→target ray; it never stretches a bone. Chains stop at 99.5% extension.

`RobotView` applies the result with `root.quaternion = Ry(yaw)·Rx(tumble)`. The displayed yaw eases toward the facing heading, so turning round passes through the camera-facing side instead of snapping 180°.

### The held weapon

`AttackVisual.collision.capsule` from `sampleWeapon` remains the only source of attack weapon geometry; trail and debug overlay use it unchanged. The player always holds a weapon, and `RigAnimator` reports it as `RigFrame.held` (segment, style, grip, `live`):

- **Between attacks** it is the weapon's rest pose (`WEAPON_REST` in `trajectory.ts`: carry elevation and natural length) carried on the chest frame, so it breathes, leans and turns with the body. The pose's `carryX/Y/Z`, `carryPitch` and `carryYaw` channels re-aim it per state: running (per weapon, `RUN_CARRY` in `poses.ts`), jumping, charging (drawn back over the shoulder), guarding (across the body), dodging, hurt, and a missed grab each carry it differently. Area moves and missed grabs (`area`, `none`) keep the carry.
- **During a melee strike** it is the simulation capsule converted to rig space — wind-up, hit frames and follow-through alike.
- **For a ranged shot** (`projectile`, `cannon`, `mortar`) it starts at the capsule's pivot and points down the line of fire at the weapon's natural length.

Changing mode inertializes the weapon as a rigid shaft — its pivot, direction and length each blend, so it turns rather than shearing or telescoping — and it never teleports: settling back into the carry is a gentle decay; entering a strike, the offset follows an S-curve over what is left of the wind-up (at most 0.12 s; 0.08 s for a shot) and is **exactly zero on hit frames**. `held.live` is set on those frames only, and `RobotView` then lays the model on the checked world capsule itself rather than the rig-space copy. The weapon is never positioned from the skeleton.

The segment becomes a `WeaponConstraint` for the hands:

- The main hand holds a point on the segment chosen by `gripOnSegment`. The grip slides along the shaft within the per-weapon range (`GRIPS` in `animator.ts`) to stay within arm reach. If the shaft is still out of reach, the trunk is carried toward it and planted legs re-sink; when the legs take the lift back it steps in under the shaft instead. A shaft the body truly cannot reach leaves the hand at full stretch toward the grip and is reported as `grip.miss`.
- Two-handed weapons (pile, lance, cannon, scythe) add an off hand at a fixed spacing along the shaft. It releases smoothly when the shaft swings out of its reach, and `grip.off` is set only while it is exactly on the shaft. Razor combo hits alternate the leading hand.
- Hands entering or leaving a constraint ease across with a decaying offset. Catching a weapon — including the next one in a chained attack — is an S-curve timed against the wind-up and is exactly finished when the hit frames begin; letting go is a gentle decay.
- Because the weapon is always held, the hands grip it in every state, including carries, shots and area moves.

Enemies have no weapon mesh. Their right arm aims at the capsule tip, or at their aim point for ranged attacks, extending through the windup. Their moves sample the same trajectory, so the arm's telegraph is now a visible cock-back. The old per-arm idle props are gone: the player's model is the only weapon, and razor keeps a gauntlet on each forearm while its claws are the weapon. Collision code and move data are untouched by the rig, so gameplay is unchanged.

### Weapon trajectory and body motion

**Trajectory** (`src/game/combat/trajectory.ts`, simulation side). `weaponPose(move, frame)` returns the weapon in the attacker's own frame (aim, extension of reach, pivot offset). Frame 0 and the last frame are the rest pose: `WEAPON_REST` elevation and natural length, pivot at the carry point. The hit window keeps the original strike arc (`sampleWeapon` computes those frames itself). Around it:

- **Wind-up (予備動作).** From rest the weapon turns onto the strike's arc (`strikeAim` extended to `p < 0`) and cocks back past the arc's start (`arcLimits.cock`), then is released into the strike. The release takes 30–70% of the startup and is sized so the weapon reaches the first hit frame at about the speed the live arc has there — no hitch and no snap. A wind-up too short for that simply has to hurry. A thrust is drawn back shorter than it is carried, so there is something to throw.
- **Depth follows time and combo stage.** A wind-up of only a few frames cocks back proportionally less. The three hits of a jab combo share their hit frames but come from different places: level, then rising, then down from overhead with a deeper draw for the finisher.
- **Follow-through (振り抜き).** It overshoots the arc's end as far as its exit speed carries, capped per trajectory (`arcLimits.follow`), decelerating over the first 55% of recovery, then settles back to rest.
- Per trajectory: thrusts draw back (and lift the point slightly) then snap out, with a small recoil dip in extension on the way home; arc-less vertical strikes (an up or down move without `shape.arc`) swing onto their fixed line and carry on past it; orbits wind back against the spin and carry on past it; back attacks move the pivot behind.
- **Authored arcs** (`shape.arc`, the player's up and down normals). `strikeAim` is `arcAim`, and outside `p ∈ [0,1]` the sweep simply continues: the wind-up comes in from before `from` and the follow-through carries on past `to`. `arcLimits` winds back a set angle whatever the sweep's size (`cock = −min(0.32, 0.75/sweep)`, `follow = min(0.3, 0.6/sweep)`), so a full spin needs proportionally little. The pivot height (`arcPivot`) holds at `pivotY[0]` through the wind-up and at `pivotY[1]` through the follow-through, blending to and from the rest pivot; the pivot does not move behind (no back-attack offset). Arcs get no thrust draw-back or recoil.

**Arc design** (`ARCS` in `moves.ts`, degrees; ground tilt and smash share one slot, aerials another). The owner found up attacks hard to land because they poked one vertical line, and wanted down attacks to mow low and lead into up attacks:

- **Up** (ground and air): a rising vertical crescent from low in front, over the head and on behind it, the hands travelling from the hip to overhead. It covers the front, the air above and a little behind.
- **Down, grounded**: a low level sweep across both lanes (arc, razor, lance), a full low spin (scythe, −90°→270°) or a vertical overhead slam ending on the floor in front (pile, cannon).
- **Down, aerial**: a vertical crescent or wheel under the fighter (the scythe's is a full wheel); it still spikes.
- Pivot heights and end angles are bounded by the body: the hands must be able to hold the shaft where each weapon is gripped, and a blade passing overhead has to clear the head.

**Launch** (`playerMove`, `engine.ts`). Grounded down tilts and smashes now launch upward (angle 1.2 rad instead of 0.28) with knockback growth ×0.7. `popsUp(move)` identifies them (down, tilt or smash, angle > 1); when the player's `popsUp` strike lands on a target that is in `hurt`, the engine raises the target's action timer to at least `POP_STUN` (0.36 s), long enough for any weapon's up attack to follow. Down aerials still spike (−1.15); up attacks keep their 1.28 launch. The `lance-sweep` orbit sets angle 0.2, so it is not a launcher; the `pile-launch` burst (1.3) still is.

**Weapon model** (`src/render/armory.ts`). Each style is drawn along +Y in metres. Solid parts — the arc's hilt and guard riding the hand, the pile's hammer head, the lance head, the cannon body, the scythe blade — keep their size; only the marked blade or shaft section changes length. A segment shorter than the natural length slides the weapon back through the hands instead of shrinking it (lance, pile, scythe). `WeaponView.place` orients it: the blade edge leads a swing and faces forward at rest, smoothed so the weapon does not roll about. Pile, lance and scythe heads ride the tip; the cannon only pushes a ram out of the muzzle when bashing.

**Body** (`src/render/rig/choreography.ts`). `strikePose` lays a strike over the stance, driven by a move clock (0–1 wind-up, 1–2 hit frames, 2–3 recovery) and the weapon's own aim, not by `attackMotion`:

- The trunk turns with the weapon's heading; a spin or a strike behind takes the whole body round, through the camera-facing side.
- `Strike.plane` carries the arc's plane (`vertical`, `level` or `null`). A vertical arc has no heading, so passing overhead or behind does not turn the body round (`RigAnimator` gives it no yaw); instead the trunk bends back as the weapon passes over and behind the head and forward as it sweeps underneath (chest and hip pitch follow the aim's angle about the shoulders). A vertical wheel on an orbit weapon is not a spin: the stance does not pivot round with the hips and the feet stay planted. A level grounded down sweep sinks the hips further (0.22 below the weapon's `sink` instead of 0.1) to get down to the blade.
- Weight loads onto the rear foot, then drives over the front one (重心移動), with a lunge step by the foot opposite the leading hand; hip height coils, then sinks into the strike.
- After the hit, a damped rebound scaled by the weapon's weight (反動).
- Thrusts throw the striking shoulder forward; ranged shots brace, then take the kick through the hips; the free hand counterbalances; the head keeps facing forward.
- Per-weapon numbers live in the `WEIGHT` table (`back`, `drive`, `coil`, `sink`, `leanBack`, `lean`, `stride`, `recoil`); `COMMIT` scales them by attack kind (a jab is mostly arm, a smash everything). `track()` is the keyframe curve helper.

Enemies use the same choreography with their pose style (`poseStyle`).

### Diagnostics

`RobotView.rigDiagnostics()` and `GameScene.rigDiagnostics(actorId)` return joint world positions (shoulders, elbows, hands, hips, knees, ankles, soles) read back from Pixi's real transforms, together with the solved `RigFrame` (including `frame.held`, the weapon segment and whether it is `live`; `frame.easing`, how far each hand is still offset while it eases into or out of a hold; and heel/toe points with `frame.feet`) and `RigSpec`. `GameScene.combatGeometry(actorId)` returns the weapon's `pivot` (root world position, style, visibility) next to its `tip`, both from Pixi's real matrices, plus trail and overlay endpoints. Browser tests compare those against bone lengths, the authoritative capsule and planted anchors. They are read-only and do not drive animation.

### Add or change a pose

1. Edit the channels in `poses.ts` (`statePose`, `carry`, `locomotion`, `airborne`). Express hands relative to the shoulder in the chest frame and feet as rig-space soles; set `plant = 0` only when the feet must leave their anchors. Re-aim the carried weapon for a state with the `carry*` channels; a weapon's running carry is `RUN_CARRY`, its resting elevation and length `WEAPON_REST`.
2. Strike body motion is `strikePose` in `choreography.ts`: change a weapon's weight transfer and rebound in `WEIGHT`, how much an attack kind commits in `COMMIT`.
3. Never position the weapon from the skeleton. Change weapon geometry in `geometry.ts` / `trajectory.ts`; change only the grip range in `GRIPS` if the hands hold it badly. Change a weapon's look in `armory.ts`, keeping the tip mesh at the segment's end and fixed-size parts fixed.
4. Add invariants to the rig tests (`tests/rig-solver.test.ts`, `tests/rig-gait.test.ts`, `tests/rig-animator.test.ts`, `tests/choreography.test.ts`): rigid bone lengths, hand on the segment, weapon always held and exact on hit frames, no foot sliding while planted, continuity across the blend, every enemy kind.

### Limits

Robots are rigid-part hierarchies: no skinning or glTF. There is no wrist joint; the hand is part of the forearm, and the weapon's roll about its shaft is cosmetic and smoothed. Reaches are gameplay values far longer than a physical weapon, so blades and shafts visibly extend during a strike; this is by design, because the displayed weapon must equal the hit segment. The up/down arcs are authored gameplay shapes too: bounding the pivot keeps the hands on the shaft, but their blades still visibly extend. Wind-up and follow-through are presentation: they deliberately do not change timing, cancels or hitboxes. Motion was tuned from staged screenshots and numeric checks, not motion capture or long play sessions. Foot planting assumes the flat `y = 0` arena floor. Collision still uses authored capsules and body boxes, not the skeleton.

## Cancels

Normals permit attack/special/jump/dodge cancels after a confirmed fighter hit and the authored frame threshold. Normal recovery and offensive (attack/special) cancel thresholds gain six frames; jump/dodge thresholds and startup/active geometry keep their original timing. Whiffs permit only a late recovery dodge when enough recovery remains. Empty/reversed windows are omitted. Smashes retain their recovery unless an acquired weapon upgrade explicitly adds a hit-confirmed cancel. Utility/enemy moves retain their recovery. Cancel eligibility is checked before discarding the old move, including offensive special cooldown, remaining jumps, one air dodge per airtime, and used up-special recovery. Up-special checks its own airtime allowance, independently of offensive cooldown. Hurt/grab states cannot bypass those gates. Only the single approved cancel action is routed to execution, even with simultaneous buttons. Every new swing gets a fresh hit set; every projectile retains its own hit set.

## Input and aerial control

`input.ts` records a direction-bearing `ActionPress` at each keyboard, touch or gamepad edge. The main loop merges these presses across render frames and consumes each edge once. Optional `InputFrame.aimX/aimZ` separates right-stick aim from left-stick movement; callers that omit aim retain movement-direction fallback. Releasing a direction before the next physics step does not erase the direction of an already-recorded press.

`action-buffer.ts` retains action presses for five 60 Hz simulation frames, including the press frame. The engine attempts them at the first legal recovery, landing or cancel frame. Hitstop freezes the buffer clock; holding a button does not generate repeated attacks. Pause/resume, nonplaying phases, restart, sector change, death/respawn and an actual player hit clear stale inputs. New presses during late hitstun may still execute when that hitstun ends. This is simulation state, not a render-frame delay.

Normal aerial attacks call the same `movePlayer` control as free movement. Jump-release cuts and downward fast-fall input also apply during a normal aerial. Authored phase/up-special/down-special and air-dodge motion stays locked to its own behavior. `WorldState.recoveryAvailable` exposes the living player's remaining up-special allowance; it is restored on landing. It is an allowance indicator, not permission to bypass hitstun or move recovery.

## Run upgrades

`upgrades.ts` owns stable upgrade IDs, weapon eligibility, bounded ranks, deterministic reward choices and the pure `upgradeMove` transformation. Resolved move geometry and cancel windows are shared by collision and rendering. Additional hit/projectile effects live in the simulation and use per-swing or per-projectile hit memory, so repeated collision samples do not repeatedly grant jumps, cooldown refunds or waves. Mutable per-run counters reset on restart and sector transition.

Stage-clear rewards are independent of the existing repair/power/stock supply. The engine, not just the UI, requires one offered upgrade before a normal completed-stage departure. The initial loadout remains a deep-copied restart baseline, so module exchanges and acquired upgrades cannot leak into the next run.

## Combat readability

The screen overlay keeps a P1 beacon and ground ring visible, adds an approximate silhouette when a foreground fighter/prop overlaps the player's screen bounds, and clamps a directional beacon to the viewport when the player is offscreen. A projected band and enemy rings mark enemies within `SAME_LANE_DISTANCE` in depth: a standard enemy's body half-depth plus `PLAYER_LANE_ASSIST` (±0.73), derived from the same constant. Inside it, a player strike that reaches the enemy in X and height connects whatever the weapon's radius; it is still a positioning aid, not a guaranteed hit: reach, height, timing and larger bodies still matter. These guides do not depend on 3D shadows or the full-effects setting.

Normal enemy labels prioritize telegraphs, bosses and nearby/hit actors, cap the visible set at six and use bounded screen-space placement to avoid each other. Full names and AI tactics are opt-in in Training. `GameScene.getReadability()` returns diagnostic state for browser regression tests; it does not control combat.

## Add a move

1. Add/adjust the pure definition in `playerMove` or `specialMove`. Use a stable ID and behavior ID; never a label condition. Every timing is a nonnegative integer, active intervals must fit the total duration. `utilityMove` rejects invalid intervals.
2. Select an existing trajectory or add a deterministic sampler in `geometry.ts`. Specify axes, grip, endpoint and radius. Keep body/prop collision on the CPU.
3. Tune its wind-up and follow-through in `trajectory.ts`: `arcLimits` (how far it cocks back, how far it may overshoot, cocked extension) and `strikeAim` for `p < 0` / `p > 1`. Never change the hit window there — `sampleWeapon` owns those frames, and `tests/weapon-trajectory.test.ts` holds a golden test that they stay bit-identical for every move without an authored arc. A new weapon also needs a `WEAPON_REST` entry, a `WEIGHT` entry in `choreography.ts`, `GRIPS` in `animator.ts`, `RUN_CARRY` in `poses.ts`, an `ARCS` entry in `moves.ts` and a model in `armory.ts`.
4. To author or tune an up/down arc, edit the weapon's row in `ARCS` (`moves.ts`): `up` / `airUp` / `down` / `airDown`, each built with `vertical(from, to, pivotFrom, pivotTo)` or `level(from, to, pivot, tilt)` in degrees. Ground tilt and smash share a slot; reach, radius, depth and timing still come from the move. Keep pivot heights and end angles inside what the body allows: the hands must be able to hold the shaft where the weapon is gripped (`GRIPS`), and a blade passing overhead must clear the head. A level sweep's depth comes from `min(1, depth/reach)`, so lane coverage changes with the move's `depth` too. An upgrade that turns the move into another shape must `delete result.shape.arc`, or the arc will still override its trajectory. The wind-up and follow-through follow automatically (`arcLimits`), and the body reads `Strike.plane`. Hit frames of an arc are not in the golden test, which covers only arc-less moves (and pins exactly which moves carry an arc); `tests/attack-arcs.test.ts` pins the arc contract and what each slot must hit (front, overhead, above-and-behind, both lanes for level sweeps, behind for the scythe spin, below for aerials, the launch data and the down-tilt → up-tilt combo through the engine), so rerun it after any tuning.
5. For a new behavior, implement its simulation side effect by ID and its pose presentation. Add all selectable slots to `moduleGuide`.
6. Add active-only, facing, lane/height/reach, swept-motion, per-target and cancel tests, plus a rendered matrix comparison when geometry changes. Trajectory shape and rest endpoints are guarded by `tests/weapon-trajectory.test.ts`, up/down arc coverage by `tests/attack-arcs.test.ts`, body motion by `tests/choreography.test.ts`, the held weapon by `tests/rig-animator.test.ts`, and the rendered pivot/tip by the browser weapon case in `tests/browser/game.spec.ts`. Run typecheck, logic tests, lint, production build and Playwright.

## Movement invariants

`DODGE` in `movement.ts` holds every dodge number. **No dodge grants invulnerability**: a fighter mid-dodge is hit by anything that touches them, so a dodge avoids attacks only by leaving the hit volume. Other invulnerability (respawn, sector entry, spawn/boot, PHASE specials, hit protection) is unrelated and unchanged.

- **Ground dash** (`dodge`, 0.36 s). A burst: launched at its peak speed, then `v(t) = v0·exp(−burstDecay·t)` with `burstDecay` 11 for the whole dash, so over 90% of the distance is covered in the first 55% of it and almost nothing is left when it ends. X (left/right; neutral uses the facing) launches at 17.2 — 1.65× the 10.4 dash-run and above the dash-run of any starting chassis (at most 14.4) — and travels 1.68, the same as the earlier 2/3-scaled dash (8 with exp(−3.6·t) friction, 1.68). Z (up = far lane −Z, down = near lane +Z) launches at 19.7 and travels 1.93, 1.7× the earlier 1.12. Diagonals use the normalized stick on those speeds. The pose is a dash with afterimage, with no tumble, leaning the way the body travels: forward, back, or into the lane.
- **Air dash** (`airdodge`, 0.32 s, once per airtime). Every air dash starts with a 0.13 s hang without gravity (`airHang`) and adds no vertical velocity: up/down select the far/near lane like the ground dash and launch at 21 along Z with the same burst profile (2.04 travelled, at least the ground lane dash); left/right keep the constant 12 along X (released Shift damps it after 0.2 s); diagonals combine the two; neutral is a hang in place. It is no longer a vertical recovery tool — that is the up special. An air dash that reaches the floor simply lands; there is no wavedash or landing slide. Its pose leans into the direction of travel like the ground dash. `RobotView` shows the shield bubble only for a real `shield` state and flickers any invulnerable fighter, with no dodge exemption — a dodge never looks protected.
- Durations (0.36 / 0.32 s), cancel windows and buffering are unchanged. The simulation's fixed 60 Hz step makes every dash deterministic and independent of the render frame rate.

Living actors clamp to Z ±3.8 immediately after displacement **before floor crossing**, and again after props and grabs. Outward velocity is removed. Rail-adjacent obstacle resolution selects a legal side rather than pushing through a rail. There is no depth ring-out. X ring-outs, downward crossing from above, under-platform falling, vertical blast zones and enemy/player recovery remain separate.

## Horde balance tuning

Player-sourced fighter damage is multiplied by 0.9 once in `hit`, covering melee, projectiles, throws and upgrade follow-ups before armor, shields and relic modifiers. Authored move damage remains the base value; prop damage and neutral environmental damage are unchanged. Boss/warden starting armor is 405/210.

Enemy launch resistance is `0.5 + 0.5 * clamp(preHitDamage / 200, 0, 1)` and the damage-growth term uses 65% of its authored value. This raises fresh-target launch and softens the ramp, including above 100%. Player launch calculation is unchanged. Weight, armor and launch directions still apply.

Dash attacks launch at 9 and decay at 8/s. Hit cancels into a normal cannot re-trigger a dash attack, and the dash-run timer is consumed on dash-attack start. Phase travel starts at 12 (cross/focused) or 8 (push/split), then decays at 14/s once the 0.28 s forced-motion window ends, on the ground and in the air.

Campaign mixed rosters gain at least three minions (roughly 50% for larger formations); the roguelite opening uses the same opening roster. Training and solo boss rosters are preserved. Formations below eight deploy immediately; larger formations retain four-unit staggered arrivals and the existing live-enemy cap. The director keeps compatible-threat and escape-lane rules, with the post-attack gap shortened to 0.4 s (single) / 0.6 s (pair), regroup time to 0.5 s, and individual enemy cooldowns reduced. Telegraph and active durations are unchanged.

## Enemy forms, chained attacks and phased midboss

- **Data-driven kinds.** `src/game/bestiary.ts` holds scale, weight, speed, reach, lane tolerance, armor, hover altitude and rush speed for `hound`, `gunhound`, `roller`, `mauler`, `jester`, `talon`, `mirror` and `gargoyle`. Older kinds keep their inline numbers; the engine consults the table first.
- **Forms.** `Actor.form` is authoritative state the renderer reads: a roller is `rolling` (cannot attack, heads for a firing spot) or `deployed` (stationary, fires, 1.8× weight); a talon is `spent` after its flurry (hovers low, cannot attack); a gargoyle is `airborne` or on foot. A roller's roll is capped at 5 s, so it always unfolds again and cannot kite indefinitely.
- **Chained attacks.** `ActorMemory.chain` lists follow-up steps. When an enemy move ends, the actor loop starts the next step in the same tick, before the director prunes reservations, so the whole chain keeps one reservation. A hit that interrupts the enemy clears the chain. Used by the gun hound's three-round burst, the roller's second volley and form changes, the jester's feint → backstab, the talon's stoop → pecks → rending kick → spent, and the mirror's jab strings. The talon's flurry continues only while each step connects.
- **Mirror midboss.** Built on the player's chassis: its moves are `playerMove`/`specialMove` of the run's loadout with extra start-up (the telegraph), 0.7× damage, 0.85× force and 0.6× growth, and the renderer gives it the same weapon. `phaseChanges` is its mode. Modes 1 and 2 each have `MIRROR_PHASE_ARMOR` armor and take **zero** launch impulse (and, having armor, cannot be grabbed or pulled). When the armor runs out it is untouchable for `MIRROR_SHIFT_TIME`, then enters the next mode; damage is wiped entering mode 2 and carried into mode 3, where armor is gone and normal launch rules apply.
- **Gargoyle pair.** The second of a pair spawns airborne. The flier casts the `breath` floor pattern (three strips marching down the player's lane from the breather) twice, then dives with the `leap` behaviour — whose velocity is now derived from the actual flight time, so it works from any altitude — and the two swap roles. Losing its armor in flight knocks a gargoyle to the ground. When one is ringed out the survivor is `overdrive`: shorter cooldowns, and it alternates between air and ground alone.
- **Poise.** A mauler in its attack state takes hits like an armored target (0.16× impulse, no interruption) from everything except smashes and throws.
